Glossary

Plain-language definitions for terms used across the site.

Core Concepts

Relative Strength (RS)
How a stock has performed compared to a benchmark (usually the S&P 500) over the same period. Positive RS means it's outperforming the broader market.
Relative Volume (RVOL)
Today's trading volume divided by its own recent average volume. Above 1 means more shares are trading hands than usual — a sign of unusual interest.
Support
A price level where a stock has repeatedly stopped falling and bounced, because buyers tend to step in there.
Resistance
A price level where a stock has repeatedly stopped rising and pulled back, because sellers tend to show up there.
Breakout
When price moves above a resistance level (or below a support level) with enough volume to suggest the move is real, not just noise.
Trend
The general direction a stock's price is moving over time — up, down, or sideways.
Higher Highs / Higher Lows
Each peak and each pullback low is higher than the one before — the pattern that defines an uptrend.
Lower Highs / Lower Lows
Each peak and each bounce is lower than the one before — the pattern that defines a downtrend.

Indicators

EMA (Exponential Moving Average)
A moving average that weights recent prices more heavily than older ones, so it reacts faster to new price action than a simple average.
ATR (Average True Range)
A measure of how much a stock typically moves in a day, accounting for gaps. Higher ATR means bigger typical price swings.
ADR% (Average Daily Range)
The average size of a stock's daily high-to-low range, as a percentage of its price — a way to gauge how volatile a stock is day to day.
Candlestick
A chart element showing a stock's open, high, low, and close for a given period. The "body" shows open vs. close; the "wicks" show the high/low extremes.
Hammer
A candlestick with a small body and a long lower wick, often appearing after a decline — suggests buyers stepped in and pushed price back up before the close.
Doji
A candlestick where open and close are nearly equal, signaling indecision between buyers and sellers.
Shooting Star
A candlestick with a small body and a long upper wick, often appearing after a rally — suggests sellers pushed price back down before the close.

Fundamentals

P/E Ratio
Price-to-Earnings ratio — a stock's price divided by its earnings per share. A common (if rough) way to gauge whether a stock is expensive relative to its profits.
PEG Ratio
P/E ratio divided by expected earnings growth rate. Adjusts for growth, so a high P/E can still look reasonable if the company is growing fast enough.
Market Cap
Total value of a company's shares (share price × shares outstanding) — a way to gauge company size.
Profit Margin
Net income as a percentage of revenue — how much of each dollar in sales actually becomes profit.

Trading & Risk

Stop Loss
A predetermined price at which a trader exits a losing position, to cap the loss before it grows.
R / R-Multiple
A way of measuring a trade's result relative to its risk. If you risked $1 to make $2, that's a "2R" winner.
Win Rate
The percentage of trades that were profitable, out of all trades taken for a given setup.
Drawdown
The decline from a peak to a trough in account value (or cumulative trade performance) — a measure of the worst stretch a strategy went through.
52-Week High / Low
The highest and lowest price a stock has traded at over the past year.
Swing Trade
A trade held over several days to weeks, aiming to capture a broader price move rather than an intraday one.
Day Trade
A position opened and closed within the same trading day.
LOD / HOD
Low of Day / High of Day — the lowest and highest price a stock has traded at so far in the current session.
Pre-market / After-hours (AH)
Trading that happens before the regular market opens, or after it closes.
FOMO
Fear Of Missing Out — the emotional urge to chase a trade because it's already moving, often leading to poor entries.

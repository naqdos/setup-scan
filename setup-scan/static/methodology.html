Methodology

How the momentum score, fundamental score, and setup detection actually work.

Momentum Score (0-100)

Combines four signals into one number: how a stock is performing versus the market, whether volume backs up the move, how close it is to its own highs, and whether it's trending. Each input is scored 0-100 on its own, then combined with these weights:

Relative Strength
30%
Distance from High
30%
Relative Volume
20%
Trend
20%

Relative Strength (RS)

How the stock has performed versus the S&P 500 (SPY) over the same period — outperformance scores higher.

Relative Volume (RVOL)

Today's trading volume compared to its own 20-day average — a way of checking whether a price move has real participation behind it.

Distance from High

How close the stock is trading to its own recent high — the closer, the higher the score.

Trend

Whether price is above or below its own moving average — a simple read on whether the stock is currently in an uptrend or downtrend.

This same structure runs at three timeframes — short-term (~2 weeks), overall (~3 months), and long-term (~1 year) — using a shorter or longer lookback window and moving average for each, so a stock can look strong short-term but weak long-term, or vice versa.

Fundamental Score (0-100)

A separate rating built from company financials rather than price action. The long-term score leans heavily on this, since a year of price trend alone doesn't capture whether a company is actually fundamentally healthy.

Growth averages year-over-year revenue and earnings growth. Valuation uses the PEG ratio (P/E adjusted for growth rate) rather than raw P/E, since a high P/E can still be reasonable for a fast-growing company. Profitability averages profit margin and operating margin. Analyst view blends the consensus rating with how far the current price sits from the average analyst price target.

The Three Setups

MA Bounce

Stock is in an uptrend, pulls back to touch its moving average, then closes back above it on a green candle — a continuation signal after a healthy pullback.

Breakout

Stock closes above its recent 20-day high on volume meaningfully above average — a sign of new buying interest pushing through resistance.

Undercut & Rally

Price dips briefly below a recent low (a shakeout) but rallies back above it by the close — often a sign of a failed breakdown and a shift back to buyers.

Each setup's historical performance (win rate, average R, trade count, max drawdown) is shown per-ticker on the scanner page, based on how that setup has actually performed on that specific stock over the past ~2 years.

Backtested results describe the past, not the future. Historical win rate and average return for a setup do not guarantee it will perform the same way going forward. Markets change, and a setup that worked well in one period can behave very differently in another. This tool is meant to organize signals and historical context — not to predict outcomes.

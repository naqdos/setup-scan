The three setups the scanner actually checks for, and the reasoning behind each.
MA Bounce
Continuation off a moving average pullback
A stock in a confirmed uptrend pulls back and touches its own moving average, then closes back above it on a green candle. The idea: in a healthy trend, the moving average tends to act as dynamic support — each pullback to it is an opportunity for buyers to step back in before the trend continues, rather than a sign the trend is breaking.
What the code checks
Uptrend confirmed by the last 10 days trading above their own 20-day EMA average. Signal fires when a candle's low touches within 1.5% of the 20-EMA, then closes back above it on a green candle (close > open).
Why it can fail
A "bounce" that immediately loses the moving average again on the next candle is a common false signal — the reclaim wasn't backed by real buying, just a brief wick. Volume and follow-through on the next day or two matter more than the single signal candle.

Breakout
Price clears a key level on real volume
Price closes above its recent trading range on volume clearly above normal — a sign that new buying interest is actually strong enough to push through a level other traders were watching (and likely have stop-losses or sell orders sitting near). Volume is what separates a real breakout from a false one that just drifts back into the range.
What the code checks
Signal fires when the close is above the prior 20-day high, and volume is at least 1.5× the 20-day average volume on that same day.
Why it can fail
Breakouts without real volume behind them ("wick breakouts") often reverse quickly — price pokes above the level intraday but can't hold it by the close, or holds for a day and then fails on the next. A breakout on unremarkable volume is a weaker signal even if the price technically cleared the level.

Undercut & Rally
A shakeout below support that fails to follow through
Price dips briefly below a recent low — often triggering stop-losses and panic selling clustered right at that level — but then reclaims it and closes back above by end of day. The break below support isn't automatically bearish on its own; what matters is whether sellers can actually keep price down there, or whether the dip gets bought back up almost immediately. A failed breakdown is often a sign that control has shifted back to buyers.
What the code checks
Signal fires when the day's low drops at least 1% below the prior 20-day low, but the candle's close finishes back above that prior low.
Why it can fail
Not every dip-and-recover is meaningful — some are just noise around a level with no real significance. This setup works best on a level other traders are actually watching (a well-established range low), not an arbitrary recent low with no real history behind it.
